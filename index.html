About

Electrical engineering, pointed at control.

I'm Basanta Adhikari, a junior studying Electrical Engineering at the University of Texas at Arlington, and a researcher at the Autonomous Control Lab.

I'm working on cascaded LQR position control for the Crazyflie 2.1 quadrotor — an outer loop that decides where the drone should go, feeding an inner loop that decides how to tilt to get there. Everything runs first in Gazebo, then on real hardware tracked by Vicon motion capture.

Alongside that I'm working with the lab's other platforms: optimal control on the Franka FR3 manipulator, learning-based manipulation on the SO-100 arm, and autonomous navigation on LIMO ground robots.

The gap between simulation and hardware is the interesting part. Simulation is honest about your math; hardware is honest about everything else. Most of what I've learned lives in that gap.

Active research

Cascaded LQR position control — Crazyflie 2.1

An outer-loop position controller designed in state-space and layered over the Crazyflie's attitude loop. Developed in Gazebo, then flown on real hardware with Lighthouse and later Vicon motion capture. Controller weights are tuned using SPSA and inverse reinforcement learning rather than by hand.

LQR control of the Franka FR3 manipulator

Optimal control applied to a 7-DOF research arm. Linearizing the manipulator dynamics about an operating point and designing an LQR regulator for joint-space tracking, validated in simulation before running on the physical FR3.
